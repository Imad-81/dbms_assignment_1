Problem Identification & Justification

The Core Problem: Five Failure Modes of Disconnected Hospital Records

Hospitals cannot function reliably on disconnected paper forms or isolated departmental tables. Here is why the relational model is mandatory.

"In conventional healthcare workflows, disconnected records create acute schedule conflicts, fragmented patient histories, bed contention, and untracked billing leakage. A single patient journey touches dozens of clinical services that must remain relationally consistent."
1. Schedule Collisions & Doctor Double-Booking ISSUE #1

Without database concurrency controls, two receptionists can assign the same specialist to two patients for the exact same 15-minute slot, leading to clinical delay and doctor burnout.

Database Solution: Composite UNIQUE constraint on (doctor_id, appointment_date, appointment_time).
2. Fragmented Longitudinal Medical Histories ISSUE #2

Past diagnoses, prescriptions, lab results, and allergies are siloed in paper files. Doctors lack a single point-of-care longitudinal view when prescribing medications.

Database Solution: Unified foreign-key cascade linking Appointments → Consultations → Diagnoses & Prescriptions.
3. Bed Allocation Contention & Bottlenecks ISSUE #3

Wards lack real-time occupancy synchronization. Inpatient beds get assigned to incoming patients while still physically occupied by patients awaiting discharge clearance.

Database Solution: Enforced single active patient per bed via state machine constraints and admission timestamps.
4. Diagnostic Orders & Billing Revenue Leakage ISSUE #4

Lab tests ordered during consultations and bed per-diem tariffs are billed on disconnected slips, causing uncollected revenue, untracked tests, and billing errors.

Database Solution: Consolidated billing relation aggregating consultations, test orders, and inpatient days with transaction integrity.

Database Theory & Rigor

Mathematical Decomposition from UNF to 3NF

Step-by-step resolution of repeating groups, partial dependencies, and transitive dependencies.

Step 1: First Normal Form (1NF)

Atomic Attributes

Rule: No repeating groups; atomic scalar attributes; unique row identity.

Decomposition: Flat hospital spreadsheets store multi-valued arrays for medicines and tests. We decomposed them into individual rows in prescription_item and test_order, splitting full names into first_name and last_name.

Step 2: Second Normal Form (2NF)

Full Functional Dependency

Rule: In 1NF and no non-prime attribute depends on a proper subset of any candidate key.

Decomposition: All 16 tables utilize single-attribute surrogate Primary Keys (e.g., doctor_id, item_id). Because no table uses composite candidate keys for non-associative records, partial functional dependencies cannot exist.

Step 3: Third Normal Form (3NF)

Zero Transitive Dependencies

Rule: In 2NF and for every X → Y, either X is a superkey or Y is a prime attribute ($X \not\to Y \not\to Z$).

Hospital Decompositions:
• doctor_id → dept_id → dept_name ⇒ Extracted department.
• admission_id → bed_id → ward_id → rate ⇒ Extracted ward & bed.
• order_id → test_id → test_price ⇒ Extracted lab_test.

Normalization Verification: All 16 tables satisfy 1NF (atomic attributes), 2NF (zero partial dependencies), and 3NF (zero transitive dependencies).

Database Engine Protection

Enforcing Hospital Business Rules at the Database Engine

Critical constraints declared directly in PostgreSQL DDL to guarantee zero invalid states regardless of client bugs.

BR-1: Zero Slot Double-Booking

No Overlapping Doctor Appointments

Guarantees that a physician cannot have two concurrent appointments on the same date and time slot.

ALTER TABLE appointment ADD CONSTRAINT uq_doctor_slot UNIQUE (doctor_id, appointment_date, appointment_time);
BR-2 & BR-3: Bed Allocation & Stay Dates

Single Active Patient Per Bed & Chronology

Discharge timestamp must never precede admission timestamp, and bed allocation is validated on insertion.

ALTER TABLE admission ADD CONSTRAINT chk_admission_dates CHECK (discharge_date IS NULL OR discharge_date >= admission_date);
BR-4: Positive Financials

Non-Negative Balances & Payment Bounds

Total bill charges cannot be negative, and payments cannot exceed the invoiced total amount.

ALTER TABLE bill ADD CONSTRAINT chk_bill_positive CHECK (total_amount >= 0), ADD CONSTRAINT chk_paid_bounds CHECK (paid_amount <= total_amount);
BR-5 & BR-6: Clinical & Domain Bounds

Vitals Range & Biological Consistency

Protects medical records against out-of-range typographical entries at insert time.

ALTER TABLE consultation ADD CONSTRAINT chk_temp CHECK (temperature_celsius BETWEEN 30.0 AND 45.0), ADD CONSTRAINT chk_spo2 CHECK (spo2_percent BETWEEN 0 AND 100);

Query 1: Longitudinal Patient History

Cross-Departmental Longitudinal Join

SELECT p.first_name || ' ' || p.last_name AS patient, a.appointment_date, 'Dr. ' || d.last_name AS doctor, dept.department_name, c.symptoms, STRING_AGG(DISTINCT diag.diagnosis_name, '; ') AS diagnoses, STRING_AGG(DISTINCT pi.medicine_name, '; ') AS medicines FROM patient p JOIN appointment a ON p.patient_id = a.patient_id JOIN doctor d ON a.doctor_id = d.doctor_id JOIN department dept ON d.department_id = dept.department_id JOIN consultation c ON a.appointment_id = c.appointment_id LEFT JOIN diagnosis diag ON c.consultation_id = diag.consultation_id LEFT JOIN prescription pr ON c.consultation_id = pr.consultation_id LEFT JOIN prescription_item pi ON pr.prescription_id = pi.prescription_id WHERE p.patient_id = 1 GROUP BY p.first_name, p.last_name, a.appointment_date, d.last_name, dept.department_name, c.symptoms;

Viva-Voce Master Guide

Anticipated Professor Viva Questions & Bulletproof Answers

Direct, precise theoretical and practical answers to the most common evaluative questions.

Question 1

Why not store medications as a JSON array or CSV string in the prescription record?

Model Defense: That would violate First Normal Form (1NF), which mandates atomic scalar attributes. A CSV or JSON array creates repeating groups, prevents foreign-key enforcement on medicine dosages, and requires inefficient table-scans for drug interaction checks. Decomposing into prescription_item achieves clean 3NF.

Question 2

How does the database prevent two receptionists booking the same doctor slot at the same second?

Model Defense: Through a composite UNIQUE(doctor_id, appointment_date, appointment_time) constraint enforced at the database engine level. PostgreSQL uses an atomic unique index check inside an ACID transaction; the second concurrent commit immediately aborts with a unique violation.

Question 3

Why use surrogate SERIAL keys instead of natural keys like national ID or phone number?

Model Defense: Natural keys can change (phone updates, international patients lacking SSN). Surrogate integer keys are immutable, compact (4 bytes vs 30 bytes for strings), guarantee lightning-fast B-Tree index traversal, and avoid expensive cascading updates across millions of child records.

Question 4

How did you eliminate transitive functional dependencies to achieve 3NF?

Model Defense: In a non-3NF table, doctor_id → department_id → department_name. Changing a department's name would require updating every doctor's row. We extracted department into its own relation, leaving only department_id as an FK. We did the same for Wards, Beds, and Lab Catalogs.
